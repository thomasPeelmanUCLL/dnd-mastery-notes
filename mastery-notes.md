# Mastery Property: Longsword & Javelin (D&D 2024)

In the 2024 D&D rules, **longsword** has the **Sap** mastery property, and **javelin** has the **Slow** mastery property.

## What "Mastery Property" means

- Every simple and martial weapon now has a **mastery property** printed next to it on the weapon table.
- You can only **use** that property if you have a class feature (or feat) that grants **Weapon Mastery** for that weapon type (e.g., "longswords," "javelins").
- The property isn't chosen separately; you pick the **weapon**, and its mastery effect comes with it.

## Longsword – Sap

- **Weapon:** Longsword (martial melee, versatile 1d10).
- **Mastery property:** **Sap**.
- **Effect (Sap):** When you hit a creature with a melee attack using this weapon, that creature has **disadvantage on its next attack roll** before the start of your next turn.

So a character with Weapon Mastery in longswords can, on any hit with a longsword, impose disadvantage on the target's next attack.

## Javelin – Slow

- **Weapon:** Javelin (simple melee/ranged, thrown 30/120, 1d6 piercing).
- **Mastery property:** **Slow**.
- **Effect (Slow):** When you hit a creature with this weapon, its **speed is reduced by 10 feet** until the start of your next turn.

Thus, a character with Weapon Mastery in javelins can reliably slow targets they hit, whether throwing or using them in melee.

## How you get to use them

In the 2024 PHB, the martial classes get Weapon Mastery at 1st level and choose a number of weapon types they're proficient with; from then on, attacks with those weapons trigger their mastery properties automatically (no action or resource cost). For example, a fighter could pick **longswords** and **javelins**, unlocking Sap on longswords and Slow on javelins.
